# drills.md — Daily Drills, Standards, and Reference Tables

**Notation:** ° marks a practitioner-sourced `[ANECDOTE]` number (see `evidence.md` Tier 5) — a
starting hypothesis to be replaced by your own logged data, not evidence.
`[INVENTED]` marks a number this synthesis produced with **no evidential basis at all**.

**Definition used by four files:** **encoding volume = minutes logged in Block 4** (`tracking.md`
line 4). Not reading minutes, not Anki minutes, not card-authoring minutes.

## Three scoring laws

1. **Score on 24-hour delayed recall, never immediate.** Training gains show up specifically in
   *durable* memories; immediate recall cannot distinguish the ones that survive. [agent-2 §1.3]
   Immediate scoring is permitted only inside speed drills, where the point is throughput.
2. **All-or-nothing per line on anything verbatim.** One error in a line scores zero for that line.
   Partial-credit habits produce approximate recall, which is worth nothing. [agent-2 §6.5]
3. **Relationship standard for concepts** — see the rubric below.

### Relationship-scoring rubric (three worked examples)

A concept item counts as recalled only if you reproduce the *relationship*, not the label. Statistics
students recalled mnemonics without corresponding conceptual performance — label-recall is the
documented failure mode. [agent-2 §5.6]

*Target item: "Competitive inhibitors bind the active site and are overcome by raising substrate
concentration; Vmax is unchanged, Km rises."*

| | Response | Score | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Clear pass** | "Competitive — it sits in the active site, so more substrate outcompetes it. Vmax same, Km up." | **1** | Mechanism and both consequences, correctly linked. |
| **Clear fail** | "Competitive inhibition. Affects Km not Vmax." | **0** | Correct label and correct fact, **no mechanism** — cannot explain *why*, so the relationship is absent. This is the failure mode the rule exists to catch. |
| **Borderline → scored 0** | "Binds the active site, so substrate has to compete. Km changes." | **0** | Mechanism present but the direction of the Km change is missing. **Adjudication rule: a relationship with an unspecified or wrong direction scores zero.** Directionality is the content. |

## D0 — Image fluency (weeks 1–2, retired on gate clearance, typically 3–6 sessions)

**Protocol:** 50 random concrete nouns, timed, one image each.

**Prefer animate, interacting images.** The supporting study (animacy improving retention among
novice method-of-loci users) is abstract-only and `[PROBABLE]`, so **score a static image as a
warning, not a zero.** [agent-2 §5.5]

**Gate:** 50/50 imaged in ≤150 s, self-rated vivid.

**Failure mode that does score zero:** imagining the *word* rather than a scene — "the letters DOG
floating." That kills everything downstream.

## D1 — Palace route drill (weeks 1–2 build, then maintenance)

Build from real, over-learned buildings. **5 loci per room°**, one item per locus, fixed
non-branching order, no two loci visually confusable. Walk forwards, backwards, and from an arbitrary
locus **with no content in it**. [agent-2 §2.1]

**Weeks 1–2 exit gate (single statement, identical in `program.md`):**
20/20 concrete words, 2-min encode, 100% at 24 h · palace recited **backwards** without error ·
**40/40 words across two palaces, 4-min encode, ≥90% at 24 h** (agent-2 Stage 2) · portfolio index
populated · personal ghosting cooldown measured.

*(An earlier draft also required random access to locus 13 in under 2 s. That threshold is
`[INVENTED]` — agent-2 specifies the drill but no timing — and is dropped as a gate. Keep the drill.)*

**Maintenance:** route cards in `99-Palaces`, Core-0.93, max interval 180 days. Two minutes a month
keeps a route. [agent-3 §6.2]

### The ghosting experiment — with its scoring rule

Write a 20-item list into palace A. Wait 1 day, overwrite with a new list, and count intrusions.
Repeat at 3 days and 5 days with different palaces.

- **Ghosting rate = intrusions from the previous occupant ÷ 20 loci.**
- **Your cooldown = the shortest interval at which ghosting rate ≤ 5%** (1 intrusion in 20).
- **Portfolio size = loci used per session × cooldown days × 1.5.**

Practitioner reference point is ~4 days°. Yours is the number that counts.

**Index or die.** One spreadsheet row per palace: name, location, rooms, loci, occupant, date
written, date free. An unindexed portfolio caps out around 10 palaces°. Global numbering (`03-2-4`)
gives random access. [agent-2 §2.5]

**Two pools, never mixed:** a **hot pool** of 2–4 journeys overwritten daily for speed drills, and a
**cold archive** of write-once, domain-segregated journeys for material you keep. Never store real
target material in a speed-drill journey. [agent-2 §2.3–2.4]

## D2 — Substrate speed drill (10 min daily, permanent)

Numbers, cards and names are **not ends** here — they are the fluency substrate that concept and
verbatim encoding run on, and the only cleanly scoreable tasks you have.

### Weeks 1–2 substitution (D2 has no content until the systems exist)

The 00–99 system is not built until weeks 3–4 and cards until weeks 5–6. **In weeks 1–2, D2 is
replaced by D1-timed:** 20-item concrete-noun lists into a palace, 2-min encode, scored at 24 h,
against a stopwatch with the target set to your previous session's time.

**Your first D2 timer target is your week-0 benchmark time × 0.9.** D2 proper begins week 3 (digits)
and week 5 (cards).

### The Foer forcing function

Set the timer to **90% of current best**. Run at that pace with accuracy allowed to degrade. When
accuracy holds ≥90% at that pace for two consecutive sessions, the target ratchets down again.
Never practise at comfortable pace. [agent-5 §7.1] Mullen's split: ~25% speed drills, ~75%
application. [agent-5 §2]

### Progression standards

| Milestone | Digits (5 min, ≥95% accuracy) | Full deck (100%) | ML Words (60 s) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Baseline | ~20–30° | >6 min is normal° | no published untrained figure — set from your week-0 score |
| Week 12 | 100 immediate, ≥85% at 24 h | <3 min° | 15–18° |
| Month 12 | 150 `[INVENTED]` | <90 s° | ~20–25 (agent-2 §3.7 calls >20 hard) |
| Elite reference (not a target) | 100 digits in <15 s | 12.74 s WR (**2017 or 2018 — lanes disagree, unverified**) | 50 in 32.41 s |

**"Anything faster than this on this timeline is being sold to you."** [agent-2 §3.7]

*Two deletions from the first draft:* a Month-6 row (agent-2 supplies only 3-month and 12-month card
anchors, so the intermediate row was fabricated), and a "200 digits @ ≥95%" Month-12 cell — that is
agent-2's **Stage 7** gate and is explicitly conditional on building PAO or a 000–999 system, which
this program forbids in year 1. It is unreachable on a 2-digit system.

**Accuracy is the gate; speed is the dial.** Sub-3-minute with errors scores zero under competition
rules.

## D3 — Error autopsy (3 min daily, non-negotiable)

For every failed item, one line: *what* failed and *why the image or locus failed*. Fix the image,
not the effort. Recurring categories: image was a label not a scene · locus confusable with a
neighbour · image static · image encoded the term but not the relationship · ghosting.

A week with zero entries means you were not working at the edge of ability — a program violation,
not an achievement. This is the feedback half of deliberate practice; Foer wrote one for every
troublesome card. [agent-5 §2, §4]

## D4 — Concept encoding (Block 4, ~60% of Block-4 minutes)

**The problem this exists to solve — restated after the round-1 audit.** Abstract material is harder
to image, and the concreteness effect itself is `[ESTABLISHED]`. But whether the method of loci
**specifically** fails on abstract material is `[CONTESTED]`, and the citation this program
originally rested on was wrong:

- **Roediger (1980) does not test it.** It is an ordering-recall study whose stimuli were
  *exclusively concrete* nouns (Paivio–Yuille–Madigan imagery > 6.0). It contains no abstract-noun
  condition and therefore cannot have produced an abstract-noun null. Two independent auditors traced
  the real source to **Foth (1973), JVLVB 12:239–245**, which has not been read in full.
- **There is direct counter-evidence.** Kroneisen & Makerud (2017, *QJEP* 70:1824–1836) found the
  method-of-loci advantage was **larger** for low-imageability words than for high-imageability ones —
  the opposite of the assumed direction.
- **The mechanism may not be imaginal at all.** Aphantasic participants benefit from interactive-
  imagery instructions as much as controls (Thomas et al. 2022); navigation ability is "not a major
  determinant" of loci success, and the method "may be best viewed as a variant of peg methods"
  (Caplan et al. 2019, *QJEP* 72:2541–2553). If the benefit is **positional and structural** rather
  than pictorial, low-imageability material is not automatically excluded.

**Consequence for this drill:** the conversion step is now the **default for genuinely
low-imageability terms**, justified by the concreteness/imageability literature that still stands —
not a mandatory universal step justified by a null result that was never demonstrated.

### Protocol

1. **Classify the structure**: list / tree / process / matrix. Match the spatial grammar — hierarchy
   → containment or vertical position; process → direction of travel; causation → one image
   physically acting on the next; contrast → opposing rooms; magnitude → image size. **The palace
   layout must be isomorphic to the content structure.** [agent-2 §5.4]
2. **Convert via the symbol lexicon.** Assemble from symbols you already own.
3. **Encode into a cold-archive palace.**
4. **Self-reference pass** — one link to your own experience or projects. d ≈ 0.45, nearly free. [agent-1 §7]
5. **Self-explain** — "this follows from X because Y; false if Z." g = 0.55. [agent-1 §5]
6. **Test at 24 h and 7 d**, relationship-scored.

### The symbol lexicon

A permanent file: **one fixed image per recurring abstract term**, reused forever, so a new fact
assembles from known parts. Invention cost is the real bottleneck at scale. [agent-2 §5.3]

> **MANDATORY WEEKS 1–2 A/B — the lexicon must now earn its place.** Its warrant was the struck
> Roediger citation, so you test it on yourself rather than assuming it. Take matched pairs of dense
> technical passages from your primary domain. Encode **half direct-to-loci** and **half
> lexicon-then-loci**. Score both at 24 h and 7 d under the relationship rubric. **Set the D4 time
> split by the result.** If direct-to-loci wins or ties on your material, the lexicon shrinks to
> genuinely low-imageability terms only and you reclaim a large amount of time. This is inner-loop
> variable #0 and it outranks the other seven.

**Also test delivery modality (new, N-2).** De Beni & Moè report that loci mnemonics **interfere with
reading** but not with listening, while self-generated loci pathways beat experimenter-supplied ones
on **expository passages** (Moè & De Beni 2005). You work almost entirely from *written* material —
textbooks, PDFs, case law — so this is a demonstrated sign flip sitting directly across your main use
case. Run one arm of the A/B with the passage **read aloud to you or via text-to-speech** before
encoding. `[PROBABLE]`, abstract-only.

**Acceptance rule (hard):** an image is admissible only if decoding the scene recovers the
**relationship**, not merely the word. A padlock jamming a keyhole encodes inhibition-at-receptor;
"SOAP" encodes nothing.

**Domain namespacing (required — you run four domains).** *Capital*, *security*, *resolution*,
*stress*, *discount* and *derivative* mean different things per field. Every entry is
`domain::term → image`, and **palace estates are domain-segregated** — a locus never hosts two
domains. This is Wozniak rule 11 (combat interference) applied spatially.

**Growth:** ~5 permanent symbols per session. Week-12 target ≥150 (agent-2's own 12-week composite).

**Intake discipline:** **one primary domain at a time** for new material; others in maintenance.
Four domains ingesting at once fragments review load — the documented route to the review-debt
spiral. [agent-5 §7.2]

### Standards — and an honest note on the timeline

| Checkpoint | Standard | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Week 4 | 15-item set ≥80% at 24 h | probe `[INVENTED]` |
| Week 8 | 30-item set ≥85% at 7 d | **probe, not a gate** |
| Week 12 | 30-item set ≥85% at 7 d, lexicon ≥150 | **both probes** — the lexicon was demoted from gate in round-1 audit, since its warrant was the struck Roediger citation |
| Month 4–6 | 30-item set ≥85% at 7 d | **agent-2's actual placement** |

**agent-2 §Stage 5 places conceptual competence at month 4–6 and prices it at 40–80 hours.** This
program supplies ~32 h of encoding time across weeks 3–12 for Stages 3–5 combined. Placing the
30-item standard at week 8 is **this program's own compression and is unsupported**. Read weeks 8 and
12 as "on track for month 6", not "complete".

**Failure mode:** label-only images that pass a 1-hour test and fail a 7-day test; re-inventing a
symbol already in the lexicon. Both are caught by the 7-day test, which is why it is not optional.

## D5 — Verbatim (Block 4, ~40% of Block-4 minutes) — TWO TECHNIQUES

### D5a — Technical / legal text (precision-dominant)

1. **One locus per clause, one cue word per clause** — the word that reinstates the whole clause.
   Function words and syntax ride on grammatical constraint. [agent-2 §6.3]
2. Element-tests encode as a **fixed ordered locus sequence**.
3. Spoken pass at full speed. **If a line stalls, the cue word was wrong — fix the cue, do not add
   repetitions.**

**Cue-word selection, worked (the criterion is: which single word, if recovered, forces the rest?):**

- *"A gift is not effective unless there is delivery, whether actual or constructive."*
  → cue **`constructive`**. Not "gift" (too general, appears in fifty rules), not "delivery" (recovers
  the topic but not the distinction). `constructive` is the *marked* term — it forces "actual or
  constructive", which forces "delivery", which forces the rule.
- *"Consideration must be bargained for; past consideration is no consideration."*
  → cue **`past`**. It carries the exception, and the exception implies the rule.
- *"The seller shall deliver conforming goods within thirty days of the effective date."*
  → cue **`thirty`**. The number is the only non-derivable element; everything else is boilerplate you
  can regenerate from context.

**Rule of thumb: cue the marked, the exceptional, or the numeric — never the topic.**

### D5b — Prose, literature, speeches (prosody-dominant)

1. **Beat segmentation** — 6–8 chunks of 10–15 words at *intention* boundaries. Label each with a
   verb: "concedes", "pivots", "lands". [agent-4 §C1]
2. **Meaning justification** — for each chunk, *why these words and not synonyms?* This is the actor
   mechanism: Noice & Noice found professional actors get word-perfect retention as a **by-product**
   of interrogating word choice, retain roles for years, and show **no general memory superiority**.
   [agent-2 §6.1]
3. **Motor enactment** — a gesture per chunk, rehearsed standing and moving.
4. **Aloud at performance tempo.** Silent rehearsal strips the prosodic cue channel. [agent-2 §6.4]

### Shared back half

5. **Cumulative snowball** — 1 → 1+2 → 1+2+3. Errors trigger re-justification, not blind repetition.
6. **Graduated cue removal** — full text → first-letter transcript → chunk-label → blank. Advance
   after two consecutive clean runs. First-letter skeletons are a **self-test scaffold only**;
   evidence for them as an encoding method is mixed. [agent-2 §6.6]
7. **Stop at first perfect recitation.** Return at ~24 h, ~3 d, ~1 w, ~3 w, ~2 mo. [agent-4 §C5]

**Cost model:** ~15–25 min per 100 words to first perfect recitation untrained, 8–12 min trained;
plus ~20–35 min spaced maintenance for 3-month durability. Budget **~40 min lifetime per 100 words**
and require a named occasion. *(Derived engineering estimate, not a measurement — replace with your
own logged figures within two weeks.)* [agent-4 §1.4]

### Standards

| Checkpoint | Standard | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Week 6 | 12-line passage, 100% all-or-nothing at 24 h | gate |
| Week 8 | 30-line passage, ≥70% of lines perfect at 24 h | probe |
| Week 12 | **12-line unfamiliar** passage, 15 min encode, ≥60% of lines at 24 h | probe |
| Month 6–9 | **30-line unfamiliar**, 15 min encode, ≥60% of lines | **agent-2's actual placement** |

**Why week 12 is 12 lines, not 30.** Thirty lines ≈ 240–300 words; the cost model above prices that
at 19–36 min *at trained fluency*, which a week-12 learner does not have. The original 30-line/15-min
week-12 target contradicted this file's own arithmetic by roughly a factor of two. agent-2 §Stage 6
calls the 30-line standard a **6–9 month** amateur mark.

**Format note:** this uses USAMC **material and scoring** (unpublished passage, per-line
all-or-nothing) with a **24-hour delay substituted for the competition's 20-minute recall window**.
That is deliberately harder than USAMC and is **not comparable to competition scores**. [agent-2 §6.5]

## D6 — General detail capture (opportunistic — logged, not scheduled)

Names, numbers, dates and situational detail arrive without warning, so this is a habit, not a block.
It consumes **no Block-4 minutes**; Block 4 is split 60% concepts / 40% verbatim.

**Names & faces — all three components load-bearing** [agent-2 §5.7]: (a) name → concrete substitute
("Bryant" → "bride ant"); (b) a **distinctive** facial feature; (c) interactive imagery binding them.
**Rotate the feature class** (eyes / jaw / hairline / brow) across a group — a feature's cue
effectiveness *decreases* with how often you use it.

**Numbers in the wild:** run the 00–99 system on sight.

**Small ordered lists:** rhyming pegs (appendix) as a scratch register, so you never burn a palace on
six items. Link/story method is **intra-locus glue and throwaway capture only** — chains fail
catastrophically, loci contain errors. [agent-2 §4.1–4.2]

**Evening sweep:** name three details you deliberately encoded today. Logged in `tracking.md` line 7.

## Two rules that decide whether this compounds

**Interleave discrimination; block vocabulary.** Interleaving is *not* universal: g = 0.42 overall,
g = 0.67 visual/inductive, g = 0.34 maths, **g = −0.39 for words, where blocking wins**. Interleave
problem types, diagnostic categories, case discrimination. Block terminology, definitions and
one-to-one facts. Applying it universally makes a large part of your curriculum measurably worse.
[agent-1 §8]

**Protect encoding — and protect review too.** Divided attention costs much more at encoding than at
retrieval (direction well replicated; the specific percentages were struck in round-1 audit as
unlocatable in any source). Single-task Block 4 excessively. **Blocks 2 and 6 run under full
attention by default** — the first version of this file said to degrade them deliberately, and that
was wrong. Degraded conditions are a fallback against skipping the rep, never for first-pass or shaky
items, and **never under verbal load**: no podcasts, audiobooks, conversation, message triage or
other reading. Word-based concurrent tasks produce large retrieval interference where digit- and
picture-based tasks produce little (Fernandes & Moscovitch 2000, 2002), and all your material is
verbal. **Every latency-scored drill — D2, cards, numbers, the Saturday tracker — runs under full
attention always**, because divided attention at retrieval preserves accuracy by lengthening latency
(Craik, Eftekhari & Binns 2018) and would silently corrupt every timed gate.

## What you are explicitly NOT building

No 3-digit (000–999) system and no PAO in year 1 unless competition becomes a real goal: ~10× the
image-learning cost for ~1.5× the digit rate. Gate before it is even discussable: Stage 3 and 4
standards held 30 consecutive days. Building 1000 images for a use case that never materialises is
the most common wasted year in this hobby. [agent-2 §3.3, Stage 7]

# APPENDIX — The reference tables (you cannot start without these)

## A1. The Major system

| Digit | Consonant sounds | Memory hook |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | s, z, soft c | "**z**ero" |
| 1 | t, d, th | one downstroke |
| 2 | n | **n** has two legs |
| 3 | m | **m** has three legs |
| 4 | r | "fou**r**" |
| 5 | l | Roman L = 50 |
| 6 | j, sh, ch, soft g | **j** reversed ≈ 6 |
| 7 | k, hard c, hard g, q | **k** = two 7s |
| 8 | f, v | cursive **f** ≈ 8 |
| 9 | p, b | **p** mirrored = 9 |

**Vowels, and w / h / y, carry no value** — they are free filler. Code by **sound, not spelling**:
"cough" = 7,8 (k-f). Doubled letters count once unless pronounced twice.

## A2. Deriving 00–99

Two digits → two consonants **in order** → the most concrete, animate word you can build with free
vowels between them.

`34` → m, r → **mare** · `71` → k, t → **cat** · `05` → s, l → **sail** · `92` → p, n → **pony**

**Build all 100 this way, never by free association.** A Major-derived set is *self-repairing* — a
forgotten image is reconstructible from the code. A free-associated set decays permanently.
[agent-2 §3.2] Then apply D0's rule: give every one an action.

## A3. The 52-card scheme (Major-consistent)

**Suit → initial consonant:** Hearts = **H** · Spades = **S** · Clubs = **C/K** · Diamonds = **D**
**Value → Major consonant:** A=1 (t/d) · 2=n · 3=m · 4=r · 5=l · 6=j/sh · 7=k · 8=f · 9=p · 10=s/z

Word = suit consonant + value consonant. `4♥` → H+R → **hare** · `3♠` → S+M → **sumo** ·
`9♦` → D+P → **deep-sea diver** · `7♣` → C+K → **cake**

**Court cards (J, Q, K — 12 cards) are people**, not coded words: pick a real person you associate
with each suit's character. Court cards resist the phonetic scheme and forcing them into it is a
known source of collisions.

## A4. Rhyming pegs 1–10 (build in one sitting)

1 bun · 2 shoe · 3 tree · 4 door · 5 hive · 6 sticks · 7 heaven · 8 gate · 9 wine · 10 hen

Use as a scratch register for small ordered lists. The Major-derived 00–99 set supersedes these for
anything over ~20 items. [agent-2 §4.2]
