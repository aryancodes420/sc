# benchmarks.md — Objective Tests at Weeks 0 / 4 / 8 / 12

## Week 0 runs BEFORE training begins. This is not optional.

Most externally-valid tier boundaries below the elite level **do not exist in the literature**.
Agent 6 could not open the normative tables (RAVLT, Corsi, WAIS-IV, ROCF) because this session's
egress policy blocked the publishers, and it refused to invent numbers. That refusal is correct, and
it has a direct consequence for you: **your week-0 scores define your own beginner/intermediate
boundaries.** Without week 0 you have no denominator and the whole scoreboard is decorative.

## The five conditions that make a retest interpretable

Violate any of these and the comparison is void, not merely noisy.

1. **Fresh random material every time.** Digits, cards, names, images, Corsi sequences and words are
   all generatable — generate new ones the night before, print them, do not look.
2. **Alternate forms where material cannot be random.** Re-administering the *same* RAVLT list at
   one or even two months still shows significant practice effects; **alternate forms abolish them**.
   Prepare **four different 15-word lists** now, one per session. Using the same list four times
   makes the RAVLT data uninterpretable. [research/agent-6 §B1]
3. **Fixed conditions.** Same time of day ±1 h, same room, same caffeine dose and timing, ≥7 h sleep
   logged, no alcohol 24 h, no hard exercise 2 h prior.
4. **Abort at stress ≥8/10.** Acute stress reliably impairs *retrieval* — it corrupts precisely what
   you are measuring. [agent-6 §A3]
5. **Record audio; score afterwards from the recording**, not live. Read instructions from a script
   or TTS, verbatim, every time.

## Strategy-on vs strategy-off

Once you have a number system, span tests stop measuring raw capacity and start measuring encoding
throughput. Run each span test **twice** — pass 1 rehearsal only, pass 2 use everything you know —
and keep the two in **separate columns, never mixed across timepoints**. Be honest that strategy-off
is an *honest-effort* measure, not an enforceable one; interpret its drift conservatively.
[agent-6 §B1.5]

## The battery — 8 tests, ~72 minutes

Run in this exact order. The ordering nests the delay intervals so no dead time is needed.

**T0 (0–3 min) — Conditions log.** Time of day, hours slept, caffeine mg + time, alcohol in last
24 h, hard exercise in last 2 h, subjective stress 1–10.

**1. Digit span (3–11 min).** TTS reads random digits at 1/s. Sequences from 3 up, two trials per
length, stop after two failures at a length. Forward, then backward. Score = longest length with ≥1
correct trial, plus total correct trials. **Run the whole thing twice: strategy-off, then
strategy-on.** Protocol: WAIS-IV Digit Span structure. *Population norms exist in the WAIS-IV
technical manual, which is not open-access and was not opened — do not quote norm values you have
not read.*

**2. RAVLT learning phase (11–24 min).** **Session-specific word list.** 15 nouns read at 1/s → free
recall, ×5 trials (record T1…T5). Then interference list B once, recall B. Then recall list A
without re-reading (T6). Score: T1, T5, sum T1–T5 (max 75), T6. **Headline indices are T5 and
delayed recall** — reliability is 0.41–0.79, so **ignore any change smaller than ~1 SD**.
[agent-6 §B1.4]

**3. Corsi block span (24–30 min).** Nine blocks or an offline digital equivalent. Increasing
sequence lengths, two trials each, stop at two failures. Score = block span + total correct.
Protocol: Kessels et al. 2000.

**4. 5-minute numbers, competition rules (30–45 min).** 5 min memorisation of freshly generated
digits in **rows of 20, 25 rows per page**; then a **fixed recall window (use 10 min and never
change it)**. Scoring exactly per competition rules: **20 points per fully correct row in order;
any error in a completed row scores zero for that row**; for the final incomplete row, 1 point per
correct digit, **half points if one mistake, zero if two or more**. [agent-6 §B2]

**5. RAVLT delayed recall + recognition (45–50 min).** ~25 min after T6. Free recall of list A, then
recognition across 30 items (15 targets + 15 distractors, half drawn from list B, half unrelated).
Score delayed recall (0–15) and recognition discrimination (hits − false alarms).

**6. Names & faces (50–58 min).** Freshly generated face–name set. Two runs: (a) Memory League
format, **60 s memorisation**; (b) longer-form, 5 min memorisation. Score = correct first names in
correct pairing. Decide your spelling tolerance in advance and hold it constant forever.

**7. Speed cards, 3 attempts (58–66 min).** Shuffled deck, timer, then reproduce with a second deck.
**Recorded score = fastest time with a perfect reproduction.** Failed attempts are logged but score
nothing — this mirrors competition scoring and kills the habit of counting near-misses.

**8. Real-material retention probe (66–72 min).** 30 cued-recall items drawn at random from material
you encoded **four weeks earlier** — i.e. between the previous benchmark and this one. Score =
% correct.

> **Test 8 is the only test that measures the thing this project exists for. Everything else is a
> proxy.** If test 8 is flat while tests 1–7 climb, you are getting better at memory sport and no
> better at your actual material — that is the transfer-disappointment failure mode arriving early
> enough to fix. The fix is the ≥50%-real-material rule, not more drilling.

### Weekly light tracker (not part of the 4-weekly battery)

Saturday, ~10 min: the five **Memory League** 60-second disciplines — names, words, images, numbers,
cards — one attempt each. This is your high-frequency signal; the full battery is your formal one.

## Targets by week

Absolute targets are drawn from documented amateur trajectories and competition standards; anything
that could not be sourced is marked and set from your own week-0 score instead of invented.

| Measure | Week 0 | Week 4 | Week 8 | Week 12 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Word list, Dresler format** (72 words, 20-min delay) | baseline (untrained mean ≈ 26/72) | ≥40 | ≥50 | **≥55** |
| **5-min numbers** (raw digits at ≥95%) | baseline (~20–30 typical) | 50 | 75 | **100 immediate, ≥85% at 24 h** |
| **Speed cards** (fastest perfect) | baseline (>6 min is normal) | <5 min | <4 min | **<3 min at 100%** |
| **ML Words** (60 s) | baseline (8–12 typical) | 12 | 14 | **15–18** |
| **Names & faces** (60 s, ML format) | baseline | +50% over wk 0 | +100% over wk 0 | +150% over wk 0 |
| **Conceptual set** (relationship scoring) | n/a — no system yet | 15 items ≥80% at 24 h | 30 items ≥85% at 7 d | **30 items ≥85% at 7 d** |
| **Verbatim** (all-or-nothing line scoring) | n/a | 12 lines 100% at 24 h | 30 lines ≥70% at 24 h | **30 lines unfamiliar, 15 min encode, ≥60% at 24 h** |
| **Real-material probe** (test 8) | n/a | ≥70% | ≥80% | **≥85%** |
| **Symbol lexicon entries** | 0 | ≥40 | ≥100 | **≥150** |
| **Digit span, strategy-off** | baseline | context only | context only | context only — **not a target** |
| **RAVLT sum T1–T5** | baseline | ignore <1 SD moves | ignore <1 SD moves | ignore <1 SD moves |

**Sourcing note.** The Dresler row is anchored to a real RCT: naïve adults went ~26 → ~62 of 72 over
6 weeks at ≤30 min/day, retained at 4 months [agent-5 §1]. The numbers/cards/words rows are anchored
to documented amateur trajectories: first-ever deck attempt >6 min is normal, ~20 words/60 s is a
good 6-month amateur mark, deck <3 min at 3 months and <90 s at 12 months are realistic
[agent-2 §3.6, §3.7]. **Anything faster than that on this timeline is being sold to you.**
Strategy-off span and RAVLT rows are deliberately *not* targets — they are context, and the
literature does not support treating them as trainable outcomes.

## Long-horizon anchors (year 2+, and a flagged conflict)

The only externally valid tiers are the memory-sport standards. **Two lanes returned different
numbers for the International Master of Memory standard**, and both cite the same family of sources:

- Agent 6: **1,000 digits/hour, 10 decks/hour, one deck ≤2:00**, plus ≥3,000 championship points
  (Grandmaster ≥5,000).
- Agent 2: **1,400 digits/hour, 14 decks/hour, one deck <40 s**.

These are almost certainly different eras of the standard — the criteria have been revised upward
over time, and neither agent could open the primary rulebook because the egress policy blocked it.
**Treat this as `[CONTESTED]` and verify against the current WMSC rulebook before using either.**
It changes nothing in the 12-week program: both sets sit far beyond it, and neither is a goal of
this system. Elite anchors that *are* firmly sourced: speed-cards world record **12.74 s** (2017),
Mullen **18.65 s** (2016).
