# Will my cat actually keep a costume on? The five-minute rule

Short answer: collars, yes. Hats and capes, for as long as you need to get the photo — and that's the point.

## The five-minute rule
A costume on a cat is a **moment**, not an outfit. Put it on, take the photo, take it off. Five minutes is plenty, and most cats tolerate five minutes of almost anything if the piece is soft, nothing covers their face, and there's a treat at the end.

Everything Catwalk Club sells is built around that: velcro or a chin strap rather than anything that has to be pulled over the head, faces and eyes always clear, and nothing rigid.

## Start with the easiest piece
If your cat has never worn anything, don't start with the spider. Start with the **Bow Tie Collar** — it's a collar, it adjusts, and it has a breakaway buckle. Once a collar is boring to them, a hat with a chin strap (the Pumpkin set) is a small step. The Lion Mane and the Santa set fasten under the chin the same way. The Devil Bat Cape sits on the shoulders. The Spider fastens under the chest like a harness, so it suits cats already used to one.

## Making the five minutes easy
- **Let them sniff it first.** Leave it on the floor for a day.
- **Treats before, during and after.** The costume predicts good things.
- **One piece at a time.** Cape first, hood later.
- **Read the tail.** Flicking, flattened ears, trying to back out — that's a no for today. Off it comes, try again another day or don't.

Thirty days to return it, **worn or not**. We'd rather you tried than didn't.
