# How to measure your cat for a costume (it takes 20 seconds)

Every size Catwalk Club sells is a **neck measurement**. Get that one number and every piece on the site — collar, mane, cape, hat — fits the way it should.

## What you need
A soft tape measure (a dressmaker's tape or a phone charging cable and a ruler will do) and a cat that's sitting still. Feeding time is a good moment.

## The measurement
1. Wrap the tape around the neck **where a collar naturally sits** — low on the neck, just above the shoulders, not up under the chin.
2. Snug, not tight. Then **add two fingers** of slack between the tape and the fur.
3. Read the number in centimetres. That's your neck size.

Long-haired cat? Press the tape gently through the fur to the skin — you're measuring the cat, not the coat.

## What to do with it
- **Collars** (Bow Tie, Bandana): check the number sits inside the adjustable range. Bandana Collar S covers 24–40cm, which is most cats.
- **Lion Mane**: S 28cm · M 32cm · L 38cm. Pick the first size your number fits under.
- **Devil Bat Cape**: S ~29cm · M ~33cm · L ~40cm.
- **Spider Costume**: measure the chest too — around the widest part just behind the front legs. S is neck 20–32cm / chest 32–42cm; M is 30–40cm / 40–55cm.
- **Pumpkin set and Santa set**: one size, adjustable.

## Between two sizes?
Take the larger. A slightly loose costume gets worn; a tight one comes straight off.

## Haven't got a tape handy?
Our [sizes by breed](/pages/sizes-by-breed) page gives typical figures for Maine Coons, Ragdolls, British Shorthairs, Bengals, Siamese and more — a fair starting point, but measure before you order if you can.
