Fit & care

Almost every bad costume experience is a sizing problem. Two minutes with a tape measure fixes most of them.

How to measure

1 · Chest girth — the important one

Wrap a soft tape all the way around the chest at its widest point, just behind the front legs. Snug, not tight — you should get two fingers underneath.

2 · Neck

Around the base of the neck, where a collar would sit.

3 · Back length

Base of the neck to the base of the tail. Only matters for jumpers and hoodies.

No tape measure?

Use a piece of string, mark it, then hold it against a ruler. A phone charging cable works too.

Between two sizes?

Always size up. A costume that's slightly loose gets worn. One that's slightly tight gets fought off in ten seconds and returned.

Long-haired cat?

Measure to the body, not the floof — press the tape gently through the coat.

Size chart

Bandanas, bow ties and flower collars tie or adjust, so they fit almost any cat without measuring.

Wearing it safely

Costumes are for short, supervised wear. Minutes, not hours. Never leave a costume on a cat who's alone, and never overnight.

Take it off straight away if your cat freezes, crouches flat, walks backwards, tries to bite it off, or stops being able to move normally. That's a no, and a no is fine.

🚫 What we won't claim

We don't say a costume calms, comforts or settles a cat. Those are health claims and we're not making them — a costume is a costume.

We also won't pretend every cat enjoys this. Many don't. If yours is one of them, a bandana is usually the furthest they'll go, and that's a perfectly good photo too.
