# Positioning — The 3-3-3 Store

> **Decided by the owner, 2026-08-05.** This is the primary position. Every page, every
> element, every piece of copy is judged on how well it advances the sentence below.
> If a change doesn't serve this, it doesn't ship.

---

## The sentence

> **Everything your rescue dog needs in their first three months — matched to the stage
> they're actually at.**

Supporting proof line (the trust story underneath it):

> **Sold by hand before it was ever sold online.**

---

## What 3-3-3 is

The settling timeline every UK rescue teaches new adopters:

| Stage | What's happening to the dog | What the owner is feeling |
|---|---|---|
| **3 days** | Decompressing. Overwhelmed, hiding, often not eating. | "Have I made a mistake?" |
| **3 weeks** | Settling. Learning the routine, testing boundaries, real personality appears. | "We're getting somewhere." |
| **3 months** | At home. Properly relaxed, bonded, theirs. | "I can't imagine the house without them." |

**Why it's ours to take:** every rescue teaches it, only charities rank for it, and **no
retailer has claimed it commercially.** It's behavioural, not medical, so it carries no ASA
risk. And it maps onto the range we already sell.

⚠️ Say *"what many rescues tell adopters"*, never *"the rule"*. There is credible pushback
against treating it as rigid — every dog differs, and saying so makes us more trustworthy,
not less.

---

## Why this beats a discount ladder

Today the bundles are three price points with three discounts. That asks the customer
"how much do you want to spend?" — and the honest answer is always "less".

Reframed as stages, it asks **"where is your dog right now?"** — a question they know the
answer to, and one that has nothing to do with money. The upsell stops being a discount
and becomes a diagnosis.

| Stage | Bundle | Price |
|---|---|---|
| Days 1–3 · decompress | **The First Days Kit** | £34.99 |
| Weeks 1–3 · settle | **The Settle-In Bundle** | £64.99 |
| Months 1–3 · at home | **The Complete Calm System** | £139.99 |

The ladder now has a reason to exist that a customer can feel.

## What it changes, surface by surface

**Homepage.** The hero stops selling a product and asks the question: *"Where's your dog
right now?"* — three doors, one per stage. That is the whole shop in one screen, and it is
instantly different from every rival's "shop calming beds" hero.

**The quiz.** Already asks what's hardest right now. Add one question: *"How long have you
had them?"* — that alone routes people to the right stage, and it's the most natural upsell
in the range.

**Product pages.** Every product declares its stage. A lick mat is a day-one product. A bed
is a week-one product. It gives every single SKU a reason to be in the basket *now*.

**Collections.** `settling-a-new-rescue` already exists — it becomes the spine of the site,
split into the three stages.

**Content & SEO.** The winnable long-tail maps straight onto the stages, and each has an
obvious landing page:
- *"rescue dog won't sleep first night"*, *"rescue dog not eating first 3 days"* → Days 1–3
- *"rescue dog settling in", "when will my rescue relax"* → Weeks 1–3
- *"rescue dog still nervous after 2 months"* → Months 1–3

**Email — the strongest play.** The welcome flow becomes a *timeline*: an email on day 1,
day 3, week 1, week 3, month 1 — each matched to what their dog is actually doing that
week. Useful enough to stay subscribed to, and it sells the next stage at the exact moment
it becomes relevant. **No competitor has anything like it**, and it turns one purchase into
three.

**Rescue-charity partnerships.** This is the position that gets us in the door. Charities
already teach 3-3-3; a shop organised around it is a genuinely useful thing to hand an
adopter, not an advert.

---

## What it does NOT change

The product copy. It is the strongest asset in the project and already speaks to exactly
these moments ("They've been home eleven days and they still haven't lain down properly").
It doesn't need rewriting — it needs **labelling by stage and moving up the page.**

---

## The test for any future change

> Does this help someone work out which stage their dog is at, and get them the right
> thing for it?

If no, it's not a priority — however good an idea it is.
