## 1. What SEO actually is

Google is not a library that ranks pages by quality. It is a machine that tries to
guess, for each search, **which page best ends the search** — the one after which
the person stops looking.

Everything called "SEO" is one of three jobs:

| Job | The question it answers | What it looks like in practice |
|---|---|---|
| **Technical** | Can Google read and load the page? | Fast pages, valid HTML, a sitemap, no broken links, structured data |
| **Content** | Does the page end the search? | Answering the actual question, fully, better than the current top result |
| **Authority** | Should Google believe *you* specifically? | Other sites linking to you, your brand being mentioned, reviews, a real named human behind it |

A new store has **zero authority**. That single fact determines the whole strategy:
you cannot win a fight where authority is the deciding factor, so you must pick
fights where content is. That means long-tail, specific, low-competition phrases —
and it means the timeline below is not pessimism, it's arithmetic.

**Realistic timeline for a zero-authority site:** long-tail phrases can reach the top
three in roughly **8–12 weeks** with genuinely good content; ecommerce category and
product pages take **4–8 months**; competitive head terms take years. **[Src: ComCreate / W3Era, 2026]**

That is exactly why the August publish date matters. An article published 1 August has
a real chance of ranking by mid-October. The same article published 1 October ranks in
January, which is worth something — but not for Bonfire Night 2026.

## 2. How a blog post actually makes you money

It does not make money by being read. It makes money through four mechanisms, and it
is worth being precise about which ones you are actually buying:

1. **Capture** — someone searches "dog scared of fireworks what to do" in September,
   lands on your guide, and now knows your brand exists eight weeks before they buy.
   This is the main one.
2. **Qualification** — the article does the persuading so the product page doesn't
   have to. A visitor arriving at a product cold converts far worse than one who has
   just read you explaining, honestly, what does and doesn't help.
3. **Authority compounding** — twelve articles about dog anxiety tell Google you are a
   dog-anxiety site, which lifts your *product* pages too. This is called topical
   authority and it is the reason to write a cluster rather than one great post.
4. **AI citation** — increasingly, the article is not read by a human at all. It is
   read by ChatGPT, which summarises it and names you. See §6.

**What a blog post does not do:** rank instantly, work without the site being live, or
substitute for product photography. All three of those are hard dependencies.

## 3. Who actually owns these searches right now

### The informational head term — "how to calm a dog during fireworks UK" **[SERP]**

Ranking pages: Pooch & Mutt (brand blog) · Frontline (brand) · RSPCA · PetMD · PDSA ·
Dogs Trust · The Kennel Club.

**Read that list again.** Five of the seven are charities and veterinary institutions
with decades of domain authority and, in the charities' case, an implicit claim to be
the definitive UK source on animal welfare. **You will not outrank Dogs Trust on this
phrase.** Not in year one, possibly not ever, and any agency that tells you otherwise
is selling you something.

### The commercial term — "best products for dogs scared of fireworks UK" **[SERP]**

Ranking pages: Canine Natural Cures (a *product* page) · PetMD · Adaptil · Amazon ·
ThunderShirt · SupplementsWise (a blog) · Avon Vets.

**This list is completely different, and that difference is your opening.** Once the
search turns commercial, the charities drop out — they don't sell anything — and the
slots go to brands and small blogs. A product page from a small brand
(Canine Natural Cures) ranks first. This is a winnable board.

### The pattern in what's winning **[SERP][Judg]**

From the content summaries the index returns, the ranking articles share a shape:

- **Listicle structure with a number in the title** — "9 Vet-Approved Tips",
  "Top 10 Calming Products". Numbers earn clicks and give the page an obvious skeleton.
- **The same five pieces of advice, in the same order** — build a den, close curtains
  and windows, mask the noise, stay calm yourself, tire them out beforehand.
- **The "start early" line** — nearly every one says begin 1–2 weeks before 5 November.
- **Vet or institutional credibility signalled in the title or byline** —
  "Vet-Approved", "Vet Approved", or a charity's name doing the same job.

### The gap nobody is filling **[Judg]**

Look at what the commercial results are actually selling: **supplements, herbal
tablets, pheromone diffusers and calming chews.** Dorwest, Adaptil, Beaphar, Canine
Natural Cures, VetriScience. Every one of them is an ingestible or a pheromone.

The physical-comfort category — beds, dens, coats, snoods, lick mats, enrichment — is
represented in these results by essentially one product, ThunderShirt, and by Amazon's
undifferentiated category page.

**Nobody ranking is selling a curated kit of physical comfort items from a small UK
brand that refuses to make health claims.** That is a real, specific, defensible
position, and it is the one you already have.

It also has a compliance advantage you should understand: every supplement brand in
those results is one CMA or ASA complaint away from trouble over efficacy claims. You
sell a bed and a coat. You can say "many owners find this helps" and be completely
safe, and — because Google's quality framework now weights first-hand experience and
trustworthiness heavily **[Src: Google E-E-A-T guidance, 2026]** — honest hedging
reads as *more* credible, not less.

## 4. Keywords: what to target, and what to leave alone

### Leave alone (you cannot win these in year one)
`dog anxiety` · `how to calm a dog during fireworks` · `dog scared of fireworks` ·
`calming products for dogs` · `separation anxiety in dogs`

These are owned by charities, vets and Amazon. Chasing them wastes the whole budget.

### Target instead — the winnable set

The rule: **the more words in the phrase, the fewer competitors and the more likely the
searcher is to buy.** Someone typing seven words knows what they want.

**Tier 1 — commercial, seasonal, publish by 8 August**
| Phrase | Why it's winnable |
|---|---|
| `fireworks kit for dogs uk` | Almost nobody sells a *kit*; the term barely has a defender |
| `best calming bed for anxious dog uk` | Product-specific + geo; charities don't rank for product terms |
| `dog snood for fireworks` | Extremely specific, tiny competition, high intent |
| `what to buy for a dog scared of fireworks` | Commercial framing of the head term — different SERP entirely |
| `dog anxiety products that aren't supplements` | Your exact positioning, and nobody owns it |

**Tier 2 — evergreen, publish through August–September**
| Phrase | Why it's winnable |
|---|---|
| `how long does it take a rescue dog to settle` | Long, specific, and you have genuine first-hand experience |
| `rescue dog won't lie down` | Symptom-specific — the way real people actually search |
| `how to measure a dog for a bed` | Purchase-blocking question; owning it earns the sale |
| `what is a snuffle mat for` | Product-education, low competition, feeds the PDP |
| `do calming coats actually work` | The sceptic's query — and your honest answer beats the sellers' |

**Tier 3 — the questions people ask before they're ready**
`is my dog scared or just excited` · `signs of noise phobia in dogs` ·
`should you comfort a scared dog` · `dog pacing at night after adoption`

### The three rules that matter more than the list

1. **One page per question cluster.** Three articles about fireworks compete with each
   other and none of them wins. This is called keyword cannibalisation and it is the
   most common self-inflicted SEO wound.
2. **Answer completely and for free.** Withholding the answer to force a click is the
   single fastest way to lose a ranking under the current quality framework.
3. **Never make a health claim.** "Many owners find…" not "clinically proven…". This is
   simultaneously the legal line, the brand line and — because trust is a ranking
   input — the SEO line.

## 5. The structure every article should use

This template is derived from the shape of what currently ranks **[SERP]** plus the
2026 requirements for AI citation **[Src: GEO guidance, 2026]**. The two now want
almost the same thing, which is convenient.

```
H1  — the exact question, in the words a person would type
      "Dog Scared of Fireworks? What Actually Helps (UK Guide)"

[TL;DR box, 2–3 sentences, directly under the H1]
      The complete answer, up front. This is the block an AI quotes.
      Do not make anyone scroll for it.

[Byline + published date + last-updated date, visible]
      "Written by Aryan Sarna, who runs The Dog Nook. Updated 3 August 2026."
      A named human with a stake is an authority signal. Use your real name.

H2  — Why dogs react this way          ← the mechanism, briefly
H2  — What actually helps              ← the substance, as a numbered list
   H3 — 1. Build the den before you need it
   H3 — 2. Mask the noise
   H3 — 3. Give them something to lick     ← each H3 is a standalone answer
   H3 — 4. Pressure — coats and snoods
   H3 — 5. Stay boring yourself
H2  — What doesn't help (and what to be sceptical of)
      ← the differentiating section. Nobody selling supplements will write this.
H2  — The timing: why two weeks matters
      ← your unique honest angle; also the section that justifies the deadline
[Product mention — ONE, about 60% down, after you've earned it]
H2  — Frequently asked questions        ← 5–8 real questions, each with a
                                          40–60 word standalone answer
[Related reading — link to exactly one other article]
```

**Why each piece is there:**
- **TL;DR at the top** — AI answer engines lift the first substantive block. Burying
  the answer means being summarised badly or not at all.
- **Question-shaped H2/H3s** — each heading is a potential featured snippet and a
  potential AI citation. A heading like "Step 3" is worth nothing; "How long before
  fireworks should I start?" is worth a placement.
- **Short, complete FAQ answers** — 40–60 words is the length that gets quoted whole.
  Longer and it gets paraphrased; shorter and it looks thin.
- **Visible author and dates** — content tied to a named, accountable human is
  weighted more heavily. **[Src: E-E-A-T guidance, 2026]**
- **The "what doesn't help" section** — this is your moat. It is the section a
  supplement brand structurally cannot write, and it is the section that makes a
  reader trust the rest.
- **One product link, 60% down** — earn it first. A link in paragraph two reads as an
  advert and both readers and quality raters treat it as one.

**Word count:** 1,200–1,800 for the guides. Long enough to be complete, short enough
to actually be complete rather than padded. Padding is now a negative signal.

## 6. Getting ChatGPT, Claude and Perplexity to recommend you

This is the newest part of the answer and, for a brand starting from zero, possibly
the most valuable — because unlike Google, **AI answer engines are not primarily
ranking by domain authority.** A three-month-old site with a clear, well-structured,
quotable answer can be cited alongside Dogs Trust. That is not true on Google.

The discipline is called **GEO** (generative engine optimisation) or AEO (answer engine
optimisation). Brands typically see measurable change in citation frequency within
**4–8 weeks**, considerably faster than Google SEO. **[Src: GEO guidance, 2026]**

### 6.1 Let the crawlers in — check this first

Three different things are happening and they need different permissions:

| Bot | Who | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| `GPTBot` | OpenAI | Collects pages for training |
| `OAI-SearchBot` | OpenAI | Indexes for ChatGPT search answers |
| `ChatGPT-User` | OpenAI | Fetches live when a user asks right now |
| `ClaudeBot` | Anthropic | Anthropic's general crawler |
| `PerplexityBot` | Perplexity | Indexes for Perplexity's cited answers |

**Action:** confirm your `robots.txt` does not block any of these. Shopify's default
generally allows them, but check — blocking costs more in lost citation than it gains
in protection. **[Src: AI crawler guidance, 2026]**

### 6.2 Add an `llms.txt`

`robots.txt` governs *access*; `llms.txt` governs *navigation* — it tells the models
which of your pages actually matter instead of leaving them to infer it from a full
crawl. **[Src]** It is a plain markdown file at `thedognook.co.uk/llms.txt`. On Shopify
you can serve it as a page or via the theme.

### 6.3 Structure content so it is quotable

The highest-impact single change is **FAQPage schema in JSON-LD** — it makes each
question-and-answer pair explicitly machine-readable, and each pair becomes a separate
citation candidate. **[Src]**

You already have `snippets/dog-nook-jsonld.liquid` emitting Product, Offer,
Organization and Article schema. **FAQPage is the gap.** Adding it to the article
template is a small change with a disproportionate return.

Then, in the writing itself:
- Lead with the answer, not the wind-up
- Make claims specific and attributable ("41% of UK dogs show firework fear, per the
  PDSA PAW Report" is quotable; "many dogs are scared" is not)
- Use tables for anything comparative — models lift tables cleanly
- Show the author, the publish date and the update date
- Keep each answer self-contained, so it survives being extracted from its page

### 6.4 The uncomfortable part: AI cites communities, not shops

This is the finding that should change how you spend your time.

**97% of discussion sources in AI shopping results come from Reddit** — against
Facebook at 12% and Quora at 2%. **[Src: cited in AEO research, 2026]** And domains
with substantial genuine community presence are roughly **four times more likely** to
be cited by AI systems than those without. **[Src: SE Ranking]**

There is also a regional handicap worth knowing: **AI is around twice as likely to send
traffic to US sites as to UK ones.** **[Src]** You are pushing slightly uphill, which
is a reason to start earlier, not a reason to skip it.

**What this means concretely.** Being genuinely useful in r/dogs, r/DogAdvice,
r/DogTraining and r/RescueDogs — as a real person who owns a rescue and happens to run
a small shop, disclosed — is likely to influence what ChatGPT says about dog anxiety
products more than your own blog will.

**The line I would hold:** participate honestly, disclose that you sell things, and
never astroturf. Fake community seeding is both against every one of those platforms'
rules and, more practically, the thing that gets a small brand permanently torched when
it's discovered. The whole positioning of this business is that you're the honest one.
Buying Reddit comments would be the single most expensive contradiction available to you.

## 7. Where the content should live

| Platform | Priority | What goes there | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Your Shopify blog** | **1 — non-negotiable** | All five articles, full text | Owned. Every SEO and GEO gain accrues to your domain. Never publish the canonical version anywhere else first. |
| **Reddit** | **2** | No articles. You, answering questions, with the shop disclosed. | Dominant source for AI shopping citations **[Src]** |
| **YouTube / TikTok** | 3 | 60–90s versions of each article, filmed with your dog | YouTube is a heavily-cited AI source **[Src]**; TikTok is where the UK dog audience actually is |
| **Pinterest** | 4 | "Firework night checklist" as a saveable graphic | Long-lived, seasonal, and the checklist format is natively pinnable |
| **Google Business Profile** | 4 | Basic listing | Cheap trust signal even without a shopfront |
| **Guest posts on UK pet blogs** | 5 | Original pieces, not reprints | Backlinks are still how Google decides to believe you |
| **Medium / LinkedIn** | ✗ | Nothing | Publishing there hands the authority to them |

**The rule:** the canonical version lives on your domain. Everything else is a
*different* asset that links back — never a copy-paste of the article, which splits
the ranking signal between two URLs and helps neither.

## 8. How you target — and how you know it's working

### Targeting is not audience selection, it's intent matching
There are three intents and they need three different pages:

| Intent | Example search | The page it needs |
|---|---|---|
| **Informational** | "why is my dog scared of fireworks" | An article. No hard sell. |
| **Commercial** | "best fireworks kit for dogs uk" | A landing page or collection |
| **Transactional** | "buy calming snood uk" | A product page |

Sending an informational search to a product page loses the ranking *and* the sale.
Most small-store SEO fails here — it's not a keyword problem, it's a page-type problem.

### Measurement — four things, monthly
1. **Google Search Console** — impressions first, clicks second. Impressions rising
   with flat clicks means you're ranking on page two: the content works, the title
   doesn't. That's a cheap fix.
2. **Which phrases you actually appear for** — usually not the ones you targeted. Write
   the next article about what you're already accidentally ranking for.
3. **Assisted conversions in GA4** — an article almost never converts on the same visit.
   Judging it by last-click will make you kill the thing that's working.
4. **AI citation checks** — literally ask ChatGPT, Claude and Perplexity
   *"what should I buy for a dog scared of fireworks in the UK?"* once a month and write
   down whether you appear. There is no console for this yet; manual checking is the
   state of the art.

### The honest expectation
- **Weeks 1–4:** indexed, near-zero traffic. Normal. Do not panic and rewrite.
- **Weeks 4–8:** first long-tail impressions. Possible first AI citations.
- **Weeks 8–12:** first top-three positions on the specific phrases.
- **October:** the fireworks articles do their work, or they don't — and either way
  they're an asset you own going into 2027, when they'll be a year old and much
  stronger.

## 9. What to actually do, in order

1. **Publish the site.** Nothing here functions until the store is live and indexed.
   Still gated on product photography.
2. **Publish the five articles** from `SEO-PLAN-AND-ARTICLES.md`, restructured to the
   §5 template — TL;DR block, your real byline, question-shaped headings, FAQ block.
3. **Add FAQPage JSON-LD** to `dog-nook-jsonld.liquid`. Highest-return single technical
   change for AI citation.
4. **Submit the sitemap** to Google Search Console the day you publish.
5. **Check `robots.txt`** allows GPTBot, OAI-SearchBot, ClaudeBot and PerplexityBot.
6. **Add `llms.txt`.**
7. **Write three more articles** targeting Tier 1 commercial phrases — the winnable
   board, not the charity-owned one.
8. **Start being useful on Reddit.** Twenty minutes a day, honestly, disclosed.
9. **Film the article versions** for TikTok and YouTube once you have product in hand.
