What does a realistic first year look like?

    The honest verdict

    £100k profit in year one is above even the stretch case. Not through lack of effort — through timing.

    You launch in August with no email list, no reviews and nothing ranked. Bonfire Night arrives before any of that has had time to compound, so you catch perhaps a third of what the season is worth to you.

    Realistic year one: £35–70k profit on £130–270k revenue, at a £100k+ run-rate by month twelve. Year two clears it.

    Bonfire Night 2026 is the rehearsal. Bonfire Night 2027 is the payday.

    Why can't I just spend more on ads?

    The ceiling

    There are about 4.1 million firework-fearful dogs in the UK and a finite number of their owners you can profitably reach in a month. Past roughly £6–8k of monthly ad spend, each extra pound buys the same order at worse efficiency.

    Which products are actually worth selling?

    Where the money is

    Contribution is what's left after product cost, card fees and a 5% refund provision — the money that pays for advertising and, eventually, you. One Fireworks Kit is worth seven Lick Mats.

    • Bundle
    • Single
    • Lost to split parcels

    The £14,000–28,000 question

    If CJ ships a bundle's components as separate parcels, you pay shipping several times over. The Fireworks Kit loses £11.00 an order; the three-item kits lose £5.50. At target volume that is £14,000 to £28,000 a year, decided by one email.

    It also means a five-parcel bundle has five chances to arrive late — which, in the week before Bonfire Night, is a refund and a chargeback rather than a delay.
