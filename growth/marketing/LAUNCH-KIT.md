**The honest angle nobody else uses:** a dog needs **1–2 weeks** to get used to a
new bed, coat or snood before it helps on the night. So your real deadline for the
customer is mid-October, and saying so is both true and more urgent than a fake
timer. Lead with it.

> "Order by 24 October — not because we'll run out, but because your dog needs a
> fortnight to decide the new bed is theirs before the bangs start."

## 1. Meta ads — angles, hooks, scripts

Budget starts at **£20/day**. One campaign, Advantage+ or a broad UK 25–55 audience,
2–3 creatives per angle. Kill anything below 1.5× ROAS after £60 spend.

### Angle A — The Fireworks Countdown (primary, Sept–Nov)
**Audience:** UK, 25–55, dog owners. Broad beats narrow interest stacks at this budget.

**Hook variants (test all 4 — the hook is 80% of performance):**
1. "Last November, my dog spent four hours shaking behind the sofa."
2. "You've got 11 weeks until the first bangs. Your dog needs about two of them."
3. "41% of UK dogs are frightened of fireworks. Nobody tells you what actually helps."
4. "I tried everything for my rescue's firework panic. Three things worked."

**Script — 30s UGC-style, phone-shot, you talking to camera:**
> *(Open on your dog, calm, on the bed)*
> "This is [dog's name]. Last Bonfire Night she wouldn't come out from behind the sofa.
> *(cut to you)*
> I'm not going to tell you a product cures anxiety — it doesn't, and anyone who says
> so is lying to you. But three things genuinely helped her.
> *(cut to each item, 3 seconds each)*
> A coat that gives constant gentle pressure — like being held. A snood that muffles
> the bangs. And a lick mat, because licking is how dogs self-soothe.
> *(back to you)*
> We put the three into one kit. If it doesn't help your dog settle, send it back
> within 30 days. But order by the 24th — she needs a fortnight to get used to it
> before the fireworks start."

**Why this works:** it opens with a real scene, refuses to overclaim (which
paradoxically raises trust), names the mechanism, and closes on a *real* deadline.

### Angle B — The New Rescue (year-round, peaks Jan)
**Hook:** "Eleven days home and she still hasn't lain down properly."
Use your genuine founder story. The First Days Kit / Settle-In Bundle is the offer.

### Angle C — Home Alone (year-round)
**Hook:** "The pacing starts the second you pick up your keys."
Home-Alone Kit.

### Angle D — The Honest Dropshipper (top-of-funnel, brand)
**Hook:** "Most dog shops won't tell you where their stuff ships from. We will."
This is contrarian, gets comments, and pre-empts your biggest objection. Ties to the
delivery-honesty module on site.

### Creative format priority
1. **UGC talking-head** (you + your dog) — cheapest to make, best performer for this niche
2. **Problem→solution 15s** with text overlay, no face needed
3. **Static carousel** of the kit contents with prices — good for retargeting
4. **Before/after-style scene** — dog pacing → dog settled (must be your real dog)

### Retargeting (turn on once you have 500+ site visitors)
- Viewed product, didn't buy → the 30-day guarantee + the honest delivery window
- Added to cart, didn't buy → the order-by deadline
- Purchased → the other kits, 21 days later

## 3. Email flows — full copy, paste into Shopify Email or Klaviyo

Email should reach **30–40% of your revenue** by month 6. Flows beat campaigns roughly
3:1 on click rate. Set these up before you spend another pound on ads.

### FLOW 1 — Welcome (triggers: signup / quiz / popup). 4 emails.

**Email 1 — immediately. Subject: `Here's your 10% — and the honest bit`**
> Hi,
>
> Your code is **WELCOME10**. It works on anything, including the kits.
>
> Before you use it, the honest bit: we sell nine things. Not nine hundred. Everything
> here was picked for one kind of dog — the anxious, the newly-rescued, the ones who
> find the world a bit much.
>
> We don't sell calming treats or supplements. We're not qualified to, and neither are
> most of the people selling them to you.
>
> What we do sell is comfort and enrichment, honestly described, with a 30-day
> money-back guarantee. If it doesn't help your dog settle, send it back.
>
> [ Have a look → ]
>
> — [Your name], The Dog Nook

**Email 2 — day 2. Subject: `What actually helps a frightened dog`**
Educational. Link the fireworks article. No hard sell. Builds the trust that makes
email 3 convert.

**Email 3 — day 4. Subject: `The three things that worked for our rescue`**
The founder story + the Settle-In Bundle. This is the conversion email.

**Email 4 — day 7. Subject: `Your code expires tomorrow`**
Real expiry only. Set WELCOME10 to genuinely expire 8 days after signup.

### FLOW 2 — Abandoned cart. 3 emails. Recovers 8–12%.

**Email 1 — 1 hour. Subject: `You left something behind`**
Show the items. One line: "Still here if you want it." Link back.

**Email 2 — 24 hours. Subject: `Any questions about it?`**
> Hi,
>
> You were looking at [product] yesterday. Two things people usually want to know:
>
> **Will it actually help?** It's a comfort tool, not a treatment. Most dogs take a
> week or two to adopt something new. If yours doesn't, the 30-day guarantee means
> you're not stuck with it.
>
> **How long does delivery take?** [X] working days, and we tell you honestly rather
> than promising next-day and missing it.
>
> Just reply if you want to ask anything — a real person reads these.
>
> [ Finish your order → ]

**Email 3 — 72 hours. Subject: `Closing this off`**
Last nudge. In fireworks season, swap the subject to the real order-by deadline.

### FLOW 3 — Browse abandonment. 1 email, 4 hours after viewing a product 2+ times.
Subject: `Still thinking about the [product]?` — include the spec block and size guide.

### FLOW 4 — Post-purchase. 4 emails. This is where LTV comes from.

1. **Immediately** — "Order confirmed, here's exactly what happens next" with the
   *real* delivery window. Sets expectation, kills 80% of "where is it" emails.
2. **On dispatch** — tracking link + "how to introduce it" tip for that product.
3. **Day 10 after delivery** — "How's it going?" Genuine check-in, no ask.
4. **Day 14 after delivery** — review request (see `REVIEWS-SETUP.md` for the copy).

## 4. Week-by-week launch sequence

**Weeks 1–2 (from publish):** Analytics on. Judge.me installed. First 3 ad creatives
live at £20/day. Post daily on TikTok. Goal: first 10 orders, first 3 reviews.

**Weeks 3–4:** Kill losing creatives, double the winner. Welcome + abandoned-cart flows
live. Publish 4 articles. Goal: 1 order/day, CR above 1.5%.

**Weeks 5–8 (Sept):** Fireworks landing page live. Countdown section on. Content push
on fireworks keywords. Scale ads to whatever last month's contribution allows.
Goal: 2–3 orders/day, email list 500+.

**Weeks 9–14 (Oct–5 Nov):** Everything points at fireworks. Countdown live sitewide.
Email sequence running. Ads at maximum sustainable spend. **Watch delivery windows
like a hawk — a late Fireworks Kit is a refund and a chargeback.**
Goal: 5–8 orders/day at peak.

**Weeks 15+ (post-Nov):** Christmas gifting angle, then the January rescue-adoption
wave. Harvest reviews. Bank the profit; do not blow the November cash on December ads.

## 5. The one-page ad account setup

- **One campaign**, Advantage+ Shopping or broad UK targeting. Do not build 15 ad sets.
- **£20/day minimum** — below that Meta can't exit the learning phase.
- **Don't touch it for 72 hours.** Every edit resets learning.
- **Judge on 3-day ROAS**, not same-day. Purchases lag clicks in this category.
- **Kill rule:** below 1.5× after £60 spend on a creative → off.
- **Scale rule:** above 3× for 3 days → +20% budget, no more.
- **Never exceed last month's contribution** (see GROWTH-PLAN §5).
